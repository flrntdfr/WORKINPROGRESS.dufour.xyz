    I wrote my first CV in 2012 to apply for a summer camp job organizing kids' science activities. We did what every true scientist do: built model volcanoes, launched water rockets, and experimented with molecular "gastronomy."
    <br><br>
    I knew early on that I wanted to work at the intersection of technology and health. I majored in biotechnology in high school and continued my studies in France, Germany, and Switzerland.<sup>1</sup> I interned at the Freie Universität Berlin and INSERM, working on chronic pain medication and artificial organ coatings.<sup>2</sup> As my passion for computer science grew, I pursued a master's in bioinformatics and data science, including an internship at the CNRS developing sequencing software for digitally encoded polymers.<sup>3</sup> With this computational background, I was able to explore privacy and sovereign systems design through the DUNE/éole project, focusing on cloud computing and blockchain.<sup>4</sup>
    <br><br>
    Today, I am a research associate at the Leibniz Supercomputing Centre and a Ph.D. candidate in AI for medicine at the Technical University of Munich.<sup>5,6</sup> My research focuses on artificial intelligence, cloud computing, trusted execution environments, and differential privacy.
